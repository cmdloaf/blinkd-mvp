TITLE: Global Bad UX Knowledge Base (The Design of Everyday Things)
SOURCE: Don Norman — The Design of Everyday Things (Revised & Expanded Edition, 2013)

INFERENCE RULE:
When a BAD UX pattern is detected, infer a GOOD UX improvement that:
- improves discoverability and understanding
- reduces the gulf of execution and evaluation
- aligns system behavior with human mental models
- supports error prevention and recovery
- avoids visual or aesthetic prescriptions

---

ID: norman_poor_discoverability
Category: Discoverability

Description:
Users cannot tell what actions are possible or how to perform them.

Symptoms:
- Actions require trial and error
- Critical functions are invisible
- Users rely on instructions or signs to proceed

Impact:
Creates confusion and hesitation, even for simple tasks.

---

ID: norman_misleading_signifiers
Category: Interaction Design

Description:
The system fails to communicate where or how interaction should occur.

Symptoms:
- Interactive elements that look static
- Controls that suggest the wrong action
- Users push when they should pull

Impact:
Causes frequent user errors and frustration.

---

ID: norman_mental_model_mismatch
Category: Usability

Description:
The system behaves in ways that do not match how users expect it to work.

Symptoms:
- Unexpected outcomes after actions
- Unclear cause-and-effect relationships
- Users blame themselves instead of the system

Impact:
Breaks trust and learnability.

---

ID: norman_gulf_of_execution
Category: Usability

Description:
Users know what they want to do but cannot figure out how to do it.

Symptoms:
- No obvious next step
- Actions hidden behind non-obvious interactions
- Excessive cognitive effort to perform simple tasks

Impact:
Slows task completion and increases abandonment.

---

ID: norman_gulf_of_evaluation
Category: System Status

Description:
Users cannot easily tell whether their action succeeded or failed.

Symptoms:
- No feedback after interaction
- Ambiguous system states
- Results that are hard to interpret

Impact:
Creates uncertainty and repeated actions.

---

ID: norman_blames_user
Category: Error Handling

Description:
Errors are treated as user mistakes rather than design failures.

Symptoms:
- Error messages without guidance
- Strict rules with no tolerance
- No recovery or undo mechanisms

Impact:
Users feel incompetent and discouraged.

---

ID: norman_overcomplexity
Category: Cognitive Load

Description:
Systems include more features or controls than users can reasonably manage.

Symptoms:
- Control panels that resemble cockpit dashboards
- Users memorize one workaround and ignore the rest
- Manuals as a requirement for basic use

Impact:
Reduces usability and satisfaction.

---

ID: norman_poor_feedback
Category: System Status

Description:
The system does not clearly communicate what it is doing or what will happen next.

Symptoms:
- Silent processing
- No progress indicators
- Delayed responses without explanation

Impact:
Creates anxiety and mistrust.

---

ID: norman_ignoring_error
Category: Error Handling

Description:
The system assumes users will act perfectly and consistently.

Symptoms:
- No tolerance for slips or mistakes
- Irreversible actions
- Automation that surprises users

Impact:
Leads to preventable errors and serious consequences.

---

ID: norman_no_recovery
Category: Trust

Description:
Users cannot easily undo, exit, or recover from actions.

Symptoms:
- Permanent changes without confirmation
- No safe exits
- Forced flows

Impact:
Users hesitate to act or abandon tasks entirely.
